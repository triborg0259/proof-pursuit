# AI for research-level and olympiad-level mathematics with formal verification (DeepMind + open prover ecosystem), state as of 2026-09-28

Scope note: all claims are dated. "Paper claim" = stated by the authors in the paper/abstract; "independently checked" = verified by a third party (IMO coordinators, Lean kernel run by an outside party, erdosproblems.com community, Tao's wiki); "press" = blog or news. Where I could only reach an abstract or a blog, I say so.

## Key question 1 — For each system: what generates candidates, what verifies, how candidates are filtered, success rates, costs, open-source status

### Takeaway
Every serious system since 2024 is "LLM proposes, Lean kernel disposes"; the differences are in the search wrapper (RL/MCTS in AlphaProof; evolutionary sketch population in AlphaProof Nexus; blueprint/lemma decomposition in Seed-Prover 1.5, Hilbert, Goedel-Architect, Aristotle). DeepMind's May-2026 paper is the key data point for a small team: a plain "LLM generates → Lean verifies → feed compiler error back" loop (their "Agent A", a Ralph loop) solved the same 9 Erdős problems as the full system, at roughly 2x the cost on the hardest ones, for "a few hundred dollars" per problem.

### Cited Findings

**DeepMind AlphaProof / AlphaGeometry 2 (IMO 2024, Nature 2025)**
- Blog 2024-07-25: combined system scored 28/42 (silver-medal standard) at IMO 2024; AlphaProof solved two algebra problems and one number-theory problem (the hardest, solved by only 5 contestants); AlphaGeometry 2 solved the geometry problem in 19 seconds; two combinatorics problems unsolved; one problem solved within minutes, others took "up to three days" (vs. 4.5 h sessions for humans); graded by Timothy Gowers and Joseph Myers; problems were manually translated into Lean by humans before the AI started; training used ~1 million auto-formalized problems and AlphaZero-style RL with a fine-tuned Gemini — [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/ai-solves-imo-problems-at-silver-medal-level/)
- Peer-reviewed publication: "Olympiad-level formal mathematical reasoning with reinforcement learning", Nature, published 2025-11-12, DOI 10.1038/s41586-025-09833-y; core is an AlphaZero-style agent that learns to find Lean proofs, trained on millions of auto-formalized problems, with "test-time RL" that generates and learns from millions of problem variants at inference time for the hardest problems — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09833-y); [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41225005/); companion Nature news piece "Mathematicians put AI model AlphaProof to the test" — [Nature news](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-025-03585-5). (I could not fetch the full Nature text — paywall redirect; details above come from the abstract, the 2024 blog and search snippets.)
- Cost datum from the 2026 paper: AlphaProof, used as a tool inside Nexus, costs "~$60 per problem (27.5 v6e TPU hours)" per query budget; and "AlphaProof standalone in tree-search mode could not solve any of the 9 Erdős problems despite 64 TPU-hour budget" — [arXiv 2605.22763 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22763v1)
- Open-source: AlphaProof model/weights are NOT released (no release found; the 2026 results repo releases proofs only, see below). AlphaGeometry (v1) code was released in 2024; AlphaGeometry 2 was not — this last point is from my own knowledge, not re-verified in this session.

**Gemini Deep Think, IMO 2025 gold (natural language, no Lean)**
- Blog 2025-07-21: 35/42 points, 5 of 6 problems "perfectly"; operated "end-to-end in natural language, producing rigorous mathematical proofs directly from the official problem descriptions"; results "officially graded and certified by IMO coordinators using the same criteria as for student solutions" (IMO President Gregor Dolinar quoted); within the 4.5-hour limit; techniques: "parallel thinking" (explore and combine multiple solution paths), novel RL on multi-step reasoning/theorem-proving data, curated IMO-specific guidance — [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/advanced-version-of-gemini-with-deep-think-officially-achieves-gold-medal-standard-at-the-international-mathematical-olympiad/)
- Verification status: independently graded by IMO coordinators (strongest form of human verification for NL proofs), but no machine-checkable certificate; the model is proprietary.
- DeepMind's NL-grading tooling: IMO-Bench (EMNLP 2025, arXiv 2511.01846) includes IMO-ProofBench (basic + advanced IMO problems with grading guidelines) and IMO-GradingBench (1000 human gradings of proofs); the paper says Gemini-based autograders "correlate well with human evaluations" and that these benchmarks were used in reaching IMO 2025 gold — [IMO-Bench site](https://imobench.github.io/); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1794/). Separately, ProofGrader/ProofBench (arXiv 2510.13888): 145 competition problems, 435 LLM-generated solutions, expert fine-grained ratings; ensembled grader reaches MAE 0.926 vs expert scores — [arXiv 2510.13888](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13888)

**DeepMind "AlphaProof Nexus" — "Advancing Mathematics Research with AI-Driven Formal Proof Search" (arXiv 2605.22763)**
- Bibliographic: Tsoukalas, Kovsharov, Shirobokov, Surina, Firsching, Bérczi, Ruiz, Suggala, Wagner, Wieser, Yu, Huang, Horváth, Ferraiuolo, Michalewski, Lockhart, Grosu, Hubert, Balog, Kohli, Chaudhuri; submitted 2026-05-21, revised 2026-06-08 — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22763)
- Headline (abstract): "autonomously resolved 9 of 353 open Erdős problems at the per-problem cost of a few hundred dollars", "proved 44/492 OEIS conjectures", "being deployed in combinatorics, optimization, graph theory, algebraic geometry, and quantum optics research"; a "basic agent alternating LLM-based generation with Lean-based verification replicated the Erdős successes but proved costlier on the hardest problems" — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22763)
- Generator: Gemini 3.1 Pro for proof generation, Gemini 3.0 Flash for rating sketches. Search: evolutionary algorithm over a population database of proof sketches; prover subagents pick parents via P-UCB; multi-turn episodes with a cap of 90 search-replace edits and at most 5 AlphaProof tool calls per episode; asynchronous generation/validation/rating — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22763v1)
- Verifier: every sketch compiled with Lean v4.27 in isolated Docker sandboxes; compiler errors fed back into the next LLM turn — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22763v1)
- Filtering / safety: "SafeVerify checks prevent axiom injection; validator ensures original theorem unchanged; global goal caching prevents redundant AlphaProof queries" — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22763v1)
- Baseline "Agent A": independent parallel subagents each running a "Ralph loop" (multi-turn LLM ⇄ Lean feedback), no shared state, stop at first proof. Result: solved all 9 Erdős problems; Figure 3 shows it roughly "half as cost-efficient" on problems #138 and #125; on 4/6 tested problems Agents A and B were within margin of error; authors attribute this to stronger base LLMs plus compiler-feedback grounding — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22763v1)
- Costs: "few hundred dollars" per solved Erdős problem; Figure 3 box plots put medians around $100–$400+ depending on problem and agent; "Per-problem inference costs exhibit high variance due to the stochastic nature" — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22763v1)
- Problem set: all Erdős problems with Lean statements in the Formal Conjectures repository as of early February 2026 (353) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22763v1); [formal-conjectures repo](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures)
- Failure modes reported by the authors: agents "frequently offloaded a problem's core difficulty into a single sorry within a helper lemma"; "Top sketches relied on lemmas marked with sorry that the agent claimed were established results… proved to be hallucinations"; successes concentrated "in combinatorics, convex optimization, and number theory, where Lean's mathematics library is mature" — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22763v1)
- Release: all Lean proofs (Erdős, OEIS, Stacks Project, "AICollaborator" problems in additive combinatorics, algebraic geometry, graph theory, optimization, quantum optics) plus human-written NL proofs mirroring the Lean structure; Apache-2.0 for software, CC-BY-4.0 for other material, CC-BY-SA-4.0 for OEIS-derived content; verify with `lake build`; the repo contains only solved problems, unsolved attempts are in companion repos — [alphaproof-nexus-results](https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphaproof-nexus-results)

### Inferences
- The generator/verifier split is uniform: the Lean kernel is the only trusted component; all "filtering" reduces to (a) kernel acceptance, (b) checks that the statement was not altered and no axioms/`sorry` were smuggled in, (c) optional LLM rating for search prioritization. This maps directly onto a Researcher → Referee loop: the Referee's job becomes statement-fidelity checking plus axiom/sorry audit, not proof checking.
- DeepMind's own baseline result (plain generate/verify loop matches the full system on solved problems at ≤2x cost) is the most decision-relevant fact: a small team does not need RL, MCTS or an evolutionary population to get value from Lean verification; it needs a strong frontier LLM, a Lean sandbox, and compiler-error feedback.
- Costs cluster: hundreds of dollars of frontier-LLM inference per research-level problem (DeepMind), ~0.4 GPU-days per problem for Seed Prover 1.5 at competition level; olympiad-level formal provers now run hours per problem, no longer days.

## Key question 3 — Erdős problems: genuinely new proofs vs literature search, and what the community said

### Takeaway
The community record (Tao's wiki, last updated 2026-06-30) distinguishes "AI standalone" (~55 entries) from "alongside literature" (~25), "building on literature" (~40), "human collaboration" (~100+), and secondary contributions (literature search ~80, formalization ~180). Tao's Dec-2025 diagnosis: AI picks the "lowest hanging fruit", which is exactly the class most likely to be already (obscurely) solved; the first case he calls genuinely new is #728 (Jan 2026). DeepMind's 9 solved problems were validated for statement fidelity by DeepMind's own experts and logged on Tao's wiki; two of the nine required correcting the formal statement after the proof was found.

### Cited Findings
- Tao, 2025-12-26 (Mathstodon): "a number of examples of Erdos problems that were solved more or less autonomously by an AI tool, only to find out that the problem had already been solved years ago in the literature" (#897, #333, #481); he is "skeptical" that training-data contamination is the full explanation; "My theory is that the AI tools are now becoming capable enough to pick off the lowest hanging fruit… 'amenable to simple proofs using fairly standard techniques'… precisely the category of nominally open problems that are most likely to have been solved in the literature"; expects this correlation "to continue in the near term, particularly for problems attacked purely by AI tools without significant expert supervision" — [Mathstodon post](https://mathstodon.xyz/@tao/115788262274999408)
- Tao, 2026-01-07: #728 "was solved more or less autonomously by AI (after some feedback from an initial attempt), in the spirit of the problem (as reconstructed by the Erdos problem website community), with the result (to the best of our knowledge) not replicated in existing literature (although similar results proven by similar methods were located)"; the original Erdős statement "was misformulated"; he finds the AI ability to "rapidly write and rewrite expositions" more interesting than the solution — [Mathstodon post](https://mathstodon.xyz/@tao/115855840223258103)
- DeepMind's own Gemini case study (Feng, Trinh, et al., arXiv 2601.22401, 2026-01-29, rev. 2026-02-05): scanned 700 "Open" conjectures with Gemini NL verification then human expert review; 13 addressed: 5 "apparently novel autonomous solutions", 8 by locating existing literature; authors warn of "subconscious plagiarism" and that many "Open" labels reflected "obscurity rather than difficulty"; a revision reclassified one solution as independent rediscovery — [arXiv 2601.22401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22401)
- OpenAI GPT-5 October 2025 episode: OpenAI announced GPT-5 had "solved" ten open Erdős problems; these turned out to be literature finds of already-solved problems (the "Open" tag means roughly that at least one professional mathematician failed to find a published solution) — search-result summary; the primary tweets were not fetched, so treat the framing as secondary ([search context](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/AI%20Contributions)).
- Tao's wiki (github.com/teorth/erdosproblems, "AI contributions to Erdős problems", last updated 2026-06-30, then frozen): categories 1(a) AI standalone (~55), 1(b) AI alongside literature (~25), 1(c) AI building on literature (~40), 1(d) AI collaborating with humans (~100+), 2(a) literature search (~80), 2(b) formalization (~180), 2(c) rewriting (~11), 2(d) computation (3); colour codes full / partial / incorrect / unverified; 11 disclaimers incl. "not a benchmark", selection bias, "some problems were misstated", "only a superficial reference", "provisional rather than final"; systems appearing: Aristotle, GPT-5.x, "DeepMind prover agent", Gemini 3/3.1/Deep, Claude, AlphaProof/AlphaEvolve, Aletheia; "DeepMind and Aristotle dominate formal proofs (Lean); GPT variants lead standalone problem-solving" — [Tao wiki](https://github.com/teorth/erdosproblems/wiki/AI-contributions-to-Erd%C5%91s-problems)
- erdosproblems.com forum "AI Contributions" thread (activity into July 2026): Will Blair built automated checks reading Lean kernel outputs (axioms, sorries, hypotheses) "without judging proof validity"; Nat Sothanaphan uses cross-checks with fresh LLM sessions; problem #650 cited as a case where a formal proof masked a gap in the informal argument; unresolved debate on crediting AI as tool vs. contributor; Peter Scholze's position against AI-generated mathematics mentioned — [forum thread](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/AI%20Contributions)

### Inferences
- Roughly: of AI-attributed Erdős "resolutions" through mid-2026, a majority involve literature or humans; the "standalone, not in literature" class exists (~55 wiki entries, ~9 DeepMind, #728, a few GPT-5.5 Pro entries) but is a minority and is concentrated on problems that were easy, misstated, or obscure.

## Key question 4 — What it realistically takes to add Lean verification to a natural-language proof loop today

### Takeaway
The loop itself is cheap (LLM ⇄ Lean 4.27 in a sandbox, compiler feedback, stop at first kernel-accepted proof), and DeepMind showed it suffices. The hard, unsolved parts are statement fidelity (autoformalization accepted-by-compiler ≠ faithful; measured gaps 3–29 points, and one SOTA model's "97%" fell to 67% under expert review), Mathlib coverage (successes cluster where Mathlib is mature), and cost/variance (hundreds of dollars per research problem with high variance).

### Cited Findings
- Autoformalization faithfulness: "Beyond Compilation" (arXiv 2606.31002, 2026): "reported accuracy of the state-of-the-art autoformalization model in the literature is 97% while its true accuracy is 67% when evaluated by a Lean expert"; across eight systems on 400 graduate-level statements "every system has a nonzero compile–faithfulness gap… 3.0 to 29.0 percentage points" — [arXiv 2606.31002](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31002)
- ShadowBench (arXiv 2608.29270, Aug 2026): 178 postgraduate/research-level Lean 4 full-autoformalization problems in eight areas; used as Track 4 of the ICML 2026 AI4Math Challenge; motivation: metrics "can accept type-correct but misaligned statements or reject correct statements written in a different formulation" — [arXiv 2608.29270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29270v2)
- Roundtrip verification/repair as mitigation: "Faithful Autoformalization via Roundtrip Verification and Repair" (arXiv 2604.25031) — existence only — [arXiv 2604.25031](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.25031)
- DeepMind's mitigations for statement fidelity: for OEIS they autoformalized 492 conjectures with Gemini and required "test lemmas verified first few sequence terms before tackling target conjectures"; all 44 proved passed "manual review" for correct formalization and prior unprovability; for Erdős two of nine statements had to be corrected after proofs revealed ambiguity — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22763v1)
- Sorry/hallucinated-lemma risk: agents "offloaded a problem's core difficulty into a single sorry within a helper lemma" and cited non-existent "established results"; hence SafeVerify (no axiom injection) and a validator that the theorem statement is unchanged — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22763v1). Community equivalent: Will Blair's kernel-output checks on erdosproblems.com — [forum](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/AI%20Contributions)
- Kernel limits: "the kernel only checks that a proof establishes a formal statement" and cannot check that the statement represents the intended problem — [arXiv 2606.29493](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29493)
- Tooling: Lean v4.27 used by DeepMind (May 2026) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22763v1); Mathlib4 >189K theorems as of 2025-02-24 — [Wikipedia Lean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lean_(proof_assistant)); LeanDojo (premise retrieval + tactic generation, GPT-f-style search) and Lean Copilot (LLM tactic suggestion inside Lean, repo updated 2026-09-18, on Reservoir) — [LeanDojo org](https://github.com/lean-dojo); [LeanCopilot](https://github.com/lean-dojo/LeanCopilot); [Reservoir](https://reservoir.lean-lang.org/@lean-dojo/LeanCopilot); Kimina Lean Server (REST API to Lean 4 built as an RL verifier) — [Kimina](https://github.com/MoonshotAI/Kimina-Prover-Preview); Formal Conjectures repository (DeepMind, Lean statements of open problems, 353 Erdős statements by Feb 2026) — [formal-conjectures](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures)
- Blueprint workflow at scale: Equational Theories Project settled all 22,028,942 implications among 4,694 magma laws, ~7 months (2 informal + 5 formalizing), >30 contributors, Lean blueprint tracking each NL statement to its Lean encoding with a coloured dependency graph; brute-force finite magmas and classic ATPs (Vampire, Mace4/Prover9) did most of the work and "were far cheaper to run" than modern AI; ~1,000 hard implications needed new constructions; one finite-magma implication (E677→E255) stayed open for months — [Tao blog 2025-12-09](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2025/12/09/the-equational-theories-project-advancing-collaborative-mathematical-research-at-scale/); [arXiv 2512.07087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07087); [GitHub](https://github.com/teorth/equational_theories)
- LeanMarathon shows long-horizon research-paper formalization is now feasible autonomously (258 lemmas, no sorry, two papers, four Erdős problems) but with a heavy multi-agent orchestration and an adversarial auditing agent — [arXiv 2606.05400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05400)
- Cost/time anchors: DeepMind "few hundred dollars"/problem with high variance; AlphaProof tool call ~$60 (27.5 TPU-v6e hours); Seed Prover 1.5 ~0.4 GPU-days/problem at pass@8×8; Aristotle free public API (as of 2026 case studies) — sources as in Q1.
- Hard-Mode gap: on identical problems LLMs get >80% answer accuracy while formal provers get <10% in Hard Mode (answer must be discovered) — [arXiv 2604.15839](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15839)

### Inferences
- Minimum viable addition for a Researcher→Referee→human loop: (1) human (not LLM) writes or at least signs off the Lean statement, or the Referee runs test-instance lemmas à la DeepMind OEIS; (2) sandboxed Lean 4 + Mathlib pinned toolchain; (3) a generate/verify loop with compiler errors fed back (DeepMind Agent A pattern), budget-capped; (4) an automatic audit: `#print axioms`, no `sorry`, statement hash unchanged; (5) the Referee's verdict "SOLVED" only when kernel accepts AND a human confirms statement fidelity. Everything else (RL, MCTS, populations) is optimization.
- Expect Lean to help mostly in number theory, combinatorics, and analysis-lite where Mathlib is mature; for a 7-hour competition the practical use is verifying small lemmas or finite-case certificates, not full research proofs, given hours-per-problem search times.
- The comparison to natural-language grading: NL autograders (IMO-GradingBench, ProofGrader) correlate with humans but give no certificate; Lean gives a certificate but only for the formal statement; the two are complementary, not substitutes.

### Gaps
- No source found giving a wall-clock or dollar figure for a plain "LLM + Lean" loop on olympiad-level problems outside DeepMind's Erdős setting.
- The Aristotle API's current rate limits/pricing (2026-09) were not verified.

## Key question 5 — Which results were independently verified vs. only claimed

### Takeaway
IMO results (2024 silver, 2025 gold for Gemini Deep Think; Seed-Prover's 30/42) were graded/certified by IMO-affiliated graders. DeepMind's Erdős/OEIS Lean proofs are publicly rebuildable (`lake build`) and logged on Tao's wiki, but statement fidelity was checked by DeepMind's own experts; community review is ongoing via formal-conjectures PRs. Most miniF2F/PutnamBench numbers are self-reported paper claims on benchmarks now known to contain defects; the Lean FRO's lean-eval is the emerging neutral venue.
