# Automatic verification, grading and refereeing of LLM-written informal proofs (state of the art, September 2026)

Scope note: every claim below is dated and attributed to a specific source. "Paper claims" are the authors' own reported numbers; I did not independently re-run any experiment. Where a search snippet suggested a fact that the primary source did not confirm, it is flagged as unverified. Terms of reference used throughout: **false accept** = a wrong proof graded correct; **false reject** = a correct proof graded wrong.

## Key question 1 — Which benchmarks measure how well an LLM grades or verifies informal proofs, and what are the best reported numbers?

### Takeaway
Five benchmark families dominate (Open Proof Corpus, ProofBench/ProofGrader, IMO-ProofBench + IMO-GradingBench, QEDBench, ProcessBench/PRM800K), plus NVIDIA's VerProof* suites. On binary correct/incorrect judgment of competition proofs the best single frontier judge reaches roughly 87–89 % accuracy (GPT-5, 2025), versus an estimated ~90 % inter-human agreement ceiling; on fine-grained 0–7 scoring, the best system (ProofGrader, o3 + reference + rubric + 5-run median, March 2026) gets MAE ≈ 0.93 points and only 78 % within-1-point agreement. Every group that has published these numbers still uses human graders for its headline results.

### Cited Findings
- **Open Proof Corpus (OPC)**, Dekoninck et al., ETH/INSAIT, arXiv 2506.21621, submitted 23 Jun 2025, revised 14 Jan 2026: >5,000 human-evaluated LLM proofs (USAMO, IMO, etc.); a fine-tuned 8B model "performs on par with the best model, Gemini-2.5-Pro" at judging correctness — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21621); code at [GitHub insait-institute/open-proof-corpus](https://github.com/insait-institute/open-proof-corpus).
- OPC judge accuracies on binary proof correctness (paper's HTML): GPT-5 89.3 % pass@1 / 90.8 % maj@5; Grok-4 88.3 % / 89.8 %; Gemini-2.5-Pro 85.4 % / 88.1 %; OPC-R1-8B 83.8 % / 88.1 %; o4-mini 83.8 %; Qwen3-235B 81.8 %; R1 80.9 %; Claude-4-Sonnet 70.6 %; GPT-4.1 61.4 %. Human judges "agreed on the proof's correctness in 90.4 % of cases", individual judge error estimated ≈ 5 % — [arXiv HTML 2506.21621](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21621).
- OPC: providing the ground-truth reference solution changed judge accuracy only −2.7 % to +1.4 % (negligible) — [arXiv HTML 2506.21621](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21621).
- **ProofBench / ProofGrader**, Ma, Cojocaru, Kolhe, Louie, Sharif, Zhang, Zhuang, Zaharia, Min (UC Berkeley, Google DeepMind, PKU), arXiv 2510.13888v2, 1 Mar 2026: 145 problems (APMO, EGMO, IMO, Putnam, USA TST, USAMO, 2022–2025), 435 proofs from o3, Gemini-2.5-Pro, DeepSeek-R1-0528, 0–7 scores by five Putnam/olympiad-level experts; 41 % double-graded, human within-1-point agreement 87.5 % — [arXiv HTML 2510.13888](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13888); dataset [HF wenjiema02/ProofBench](https://huggingface.co/datasets/wenjiema02/ProofBench).
- ProofGrader (o3 backbone + reference solution + LLM-generated marking scheme + median of 5 runs): MAE 0.926 on 0–7, within-1-point 77.7 %, Kendall τ 0.540, RMSE 1.185; single-pass o3 with context MAE 0.964; gpt-4o with context MAE 2.197. Effect sizes: "backbone ≫ context ≫ instruction" — [arXiv HTML 2510.13888](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13888).
- ProofGrader in best-of-16 selection reached 4.14/7 vs 2.48 for a naive binary evaluator and 4.62 for the human oracle ("closing 78 % of the gap") — [arXiv HTML 2510.13888](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13888).
- **IMO-Bench / IMO-ProofBench / IMO-GradingBench**, Luong et al. (Google DeepMind, 20 authors), arXiv 2511.01846, 3 Nov 2025 (EMNLP 2025): IMO-ProofBench has 60 proof problems (basic + advanced sets); ProofAutoGrader = Gemini 2.5 Pro with problem, candidate, reference solution and grading guidelines; rubric Correct(7)/Almost(6)/Partial(1–4)/Incorrect(0), humans free to give any 0–7 integer — [arXiv 2511.01846](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.01846); [imobench.github.io](https://imobench.github.io/).
- ProofAutoGrader Pearson correlation with human grades (aggregated per model): 0.96 basic, 0.93 advanced (14 public models, 840 solutions), 0.87 on 170 internal systems; confusion matrix shows most disagreements are Incorrect vs Partial — [arXiv HTML 2511.01846v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01846v1).
- IMO-GradingBench: 1,000 (problem, proposed solution, human 0–7 grade) triples. Reported grading accuracy / MAE: o3 54.0 % / 20.2 %; Gemini 2.5 Deep Think 52.5 % / 20.5 %; Gemini Deep Think (IMO Gold) 50.2 % / 18.4 % (units as printed by the paper) — [arXiv HTML 2511.01846v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01846v1).
- Paper's own caveat: "Automatic evaluation for informal proofs is a highly intricate task, and current systems are not yet a perfect substitute for human experts", so all primary results are human-graded; ProofAutoGrader shows "occasional failure to identify high-level logical errors" and is "overly punitive for minor formatting issues or unconventional yet correct solutions" — [arXiv HTML 2511.01846v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01846v1).
- **QEDBench**, Gonzalez, Amiri Bavandpour, Ye, Zhang + 47 co-authors, arXiv 2602.20629, submitted 24 Feb 2026 (v-final 6 Jul 2026): university-level proofs, 7 judges × 5 solvers × 1,000+ proofs, >1,000 human-evaluation hours, "dual-rubric" (course rubric vs expert common-knowledge criteria). Best judge Gemini 3.0 Pro at 0.91 average alignment; all frontier judges show a "significant positive bias" (score inflation +0.18 to +0.36 on the normalized scale); reasoning models degrade sharply on Discrete Math and Graph Theory — [arXiv 2602.20629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20629).
- **NVIDIA generative-verifier suites**, Mahdavi, Kisacanin, Toshniwal, Du, Moshkov, Armstrong, Liao, Thrampoulidis, Gitman, arXiv 2511.13027, 17 Nov 2025: VerProofArena (204 USAMO/IMO/IMC-2025 proofs, human labels), VerProofBench (429 proofs, 7-point), VerOPC (3K train / 292 test), plus selection sets. A trivial MLP reaches 75.34 % on VerOPC by exploiting label imbalance; human accuracy estimated 90.4 % ("noise ceiling"); GRPO training of Qwen3-30B mainly improved detection of *stylistic* flaws, not mathematics — [arXiv HTML 2511.13027v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13027v1).
- **ProcessBench**, Qwen team, arXiv 2412.06559, 9 Dec 2024 (v-final 26 May 2025): 3,400 step-annotated competition/olympiad solutions; task = find earliest erroneous step or declare all correct. Existing PRMs (trained on synthetic step labels) "typically fail to generalize" beyond GSM8K/MATH and underperform both prompted critic models and a PRM fine-tuned on human-labelled PRM800K; o1-mini was the best critic, QwQ-32B-Preview ≈ GPT-4o — [arXiv 2412.06559](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06559).
- **PRM800K** (OpenAI "Let's Verify Step by Step", 2023) is the human step-label dataset on MATH that ProcessBench uses as the reference PRM training set — [arXiv 2412.06559](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06559) (description via ProcessBench).
- **ProofVerifier**, Yang, Wang, Kang, Yang et al. (PKU), arXiv 2602.02377v3, 16 Sep 2026: labels built by 5 independent judgments from 3 frontier LLMs, kept only on unanimous agreement, then slice-level human audit (0.5–2.5 % sampled, agreement thresholds 75/80/90 % over three rounds, <100 human hours, ">12× reduction" vs ~1,200 h full annotation). ProofVerifier-32B 82.4 Avg@8 on a 141-positive/97-negative test set; DeepSeek-V3.1 and GPT-5-mini ≈ 70–74 %; ProofVerifier-8B 76.8 % — [arXiv HTML 2602.02377](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02377).
- **MathArena "Proof or Bluff"** (USAMO 2025), Petrov, Dekoninck, Baltadzhiev, Drencheva, Minchev, Balunović, Jovanović, Vechev, ETH/INSAIT, spring 2025: with o3-mini and Claude 3.7 as graders (given grading scheme + verified solution + example evaluation), automated totals were 19.6 vs human 10.1 (Gemini 2.5 Pro), 19.3 vs 2.0 (R1), 23.8 vs 1.2 (QwQ), 19.5 vs 0.9 (o3-mini): "neither model accurately graded the solutions, consistently overestimating their quality… inflating the scores by a factor of up to 20" — [usamo_report.pdf, §3.3, Table 2](https://files.sri.inf.ethz.ch/matharena/usamo_report.pdf).
- **MathArena IMO 2025** page: four IMO-level human judges, each response anonymized and graded independently by two judges, interface from the Open Proof Corpus project; best model Gemini 2.5 Pro 31 % (13/42). Best-of-32 selection used an LLM-as-judge selection stage before human grading; judges noted "bogus citations" and short answers lacking justification — [matharena.ai/imo](https://matharena.ai/imo/).
- MathArena platform paper: Dekoninck, Jovanović, Gehrunger, Rögnvaldsson, Petrov, Sun, Vechev, arXiv 2605.00674, 1 May 2026 (rev. 15 May 2026), covers proof competitions, arXiv-level problems and Lean — [arXiv 2605.00674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00674).
- **Epoch AI FrontierMath Tier 4** (research-level): Epoch used manual grading; smaller LLM verifiers were "either insufficiently accurate or significantly biased in favor of certain models over others", so Epoch "decided against using LLMs as correctness judges" for proofs — [epoch.ai/frontiermath/tiers-1-4](https://epoch.ai/frontiermath/tiers-1-4) (as summarised in search; see Gaps).
- **RefGrader**, Mahdavi et al. (Penn State et al.), arXiv 2510.09021, 10 Oct 2025: agentic pipeline extracts reference solutions and derives problem-specific rubrics; datasets = 90 Gemini-2.5-Pro solutions (1–4 scale with error annotations) + MathArena IMO/USAMO 2025 sets (0–7). Finding: models "reliably flag incorrect (including subtly incorrect) solutions" but are inconsistent on partial credit; agentic workflow improves human agreement — [arXiv 2510.09021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09021).
- **Consequence-based, oracle-free grading**, Son et al., arXiv 2602.06291, 6 Feb 2026: scores a candidate solution by how much it helps as an in-context exemplar on related verifiable problems; GPT-OSS-120B Acc@1 67.2 → 76.3 %, AUC 71.4 → 79.6 %; claims to beat reward models, generative RMs and LLM judges on ranking — [arXiv 2602.06291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06291).
- **Strict step-level verification on FirstProof**, Yifeng Sun, arXiv 2606.10799, 9 Jun 2026: 21-proof adversarial suite (valid vs explicitly invalid) drawn from FirstProof releases, Aletheia outputs and OpenAI submissions; per-step context + restricted theorem sources localises subtle errors where global prompting fails; residual rejections are "pedantic hyper-rigor" over unstated conventions — [arXiv 2606.10799](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10799); FirstProof batches released 5 Feb 2026 and 14 Mar 2026 — [1stproof.org](https://1stproof.org/).
- **ProofRank** (quality beyond correctness), Petrov, Dekoninck, Dimitrov, Vechev, arXiv 2605.10379v1, 11 May 2026: five metrics (conciseness, computational ease, cognitive simplicity, diversity, adaptivity) over 382 problems; correctness check "93.5 % accuracy" against GPT-5.4 judgments on 200 sampled solutions; position bias handled by randomization + Bradley–Terry intercept — [arXiv HTML 2605.10379v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10379v1).
- **Mathematical Proof as a Litmus Test**, Guo, Liu, Fan, He, Li, Li, Wang, arXiv 2506.17114v4, 9 Dec 2025: human–LLM judge agreement MCC 87.61 % on their annotated proofs — [arXiv HTML 2506.17114](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17114).

### Inferences
- The "best number" depends on the task: binary correctness on competition proofs ≈ 87–91 % (near the ~90 % human ceiling, so residual disagreement is partly label noise); fine-grained partial credit is much weaker (78 % within 1 point; IMO-GradingBench exact-grade accuracy ≈ 50–54 %). A two-judge design should therefore aim at the binary "accept / name first unjustified step" decision and treat partial credit as secondary.
- Correlation figures (0.93–0.96) in IMO-Bench are computed on *per-model aggregate* scores, which hides per-proof errors; per-proof metrics (OPC accuracy, ProofGrader MAE) are the relevant ones for a referee that accepts or rejects a single submission.
- Reference solutions help fine-grained scoring (ProofGrader: >60 % of no-context cases overestimate, +1.7 points on 0–2 proofs) but barely help binary correctness (OPC ±3 %). A research-competition referee usually has no reference, so it should expect OPC-like rather than ProofGrader-like conditions.

### Gaps
- Epoch AI's statement on LLM verifiers came through a search summary of the FrontierMath page; I did not fetch the exact page text, so quote it as paraphrase.
- I found no dedicated 2026 benchmark named "MathOlympiadBench" for grading; the term in the brief may refer to IMO-Bench or ProofBench. ProofNet and NaturalProofs are formalisation/retrieval datasets (2022–2023), not grading benchmarks, and no 2026 grading results on them surfaced.
- QEDBench false-accept / false-reject split not available from the abstract; only the +0.18…+0.36 inflation.

## Key question 2 — False-accept rate of strong models on subtly wrong proofs, and effect of multiple judges, rubrics, step-level checking

### Takeaway
On hard olympiad proofs the best 2025 judge (GPT-5, high thinking) still accepted 7 of 48 flawed proofs (≈ 15 % false accept); weaker judges given rubrics inflated USAMO scores up to 20×. Majority voting over 5–32 samples adds 1–3 points; ensembling/median reduces variance; step-level, source-constrained checking catches subtle errors that global prompting misses but raises false rejects via hyper-rigor; judges are measurably worse on their own model family's proofs.

### Cited Findings
- GPT-5 (high thinking) "judged 7 of 48 mathematically flawed proofs as correct on hard USAMO problems (87.2 % accuracy)"; "current models often reward stylistic or procedural correctness rather than mathematical validity" — [arXiv HTML 2511.13027v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13027v1).
- Majority voting over judge samples "saturates around 32 majority-voted judgments per proof"; a knockout tournament + LLM-judge hybrid reached 96.05 % on Challenge-19 vs 82.23 % majority voting, and 100 % on AIME-2025 with 752 calls/problem vs 8,448 for pure judge — [arXiv HTML 2511.13027v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13027v1).
- OPC: maj@5 improves judge accuracy by ~1.5–4 points (e.g. Gemini-2.5-Pro 85.4 → 88.1 %); "All models except Qwen3-235B-A22B perform worse when judging their own proofs" — [arXiv HTML 2506.21621](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21621).
- ProofGrader: median of 5 runs gives the lowest MAE; over-crediting failures (10.8 % of failure cases) come from "surface-level heuristics, rewarding rubric-matching structure, misclassifying logical errors as minor issues"; under-crediting (12.2 %) from "penalizing early missteps, double-counting flaws, demanding excessive micro-justifications"; Algebra and Geometry each ≈ 25 % of failures; Gemini and R1 "overscore their own responses", each evaluator has highest MAE on its own family — [arXiv HTML 2510.13888](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13888).
- ProofGrader: marking schemes regenerated or produced by a different model degraded accuracy, i.e. rubric quality/alignment matters — [arXiv HTML 2510.13888](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13888).
- USAMO 2025 automated grading with a human-written scheme and verified solution still inflated scores "by a factor of up to 20"; chaotic multi-solution outputs were graded *lower*, simple outputs *higher* — [usamo_report.pdf §3.3](https://files.sri.inf.ethz.ch/matharena/usamo_report.pdf).
- QEDBench: all frontier judges show positive score inflation +0.18 to +0.36 — [arXiv 2602.20629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20629).
- Step-level verification (FirstProof suite): global evaluation suffers "context poisoning, in which superficially plausible statements mask subtle logical flaws"; strict step-level checking with constrained theorem sources localises them, but remaining false rejects are "pedantic hyper-rigor" over unstated conventions — [arXiv 2606.10799](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10799).
- ProcessBench: PRMs trained on synthetic step labels fail on olympiad-level problems; prompted critic LLMs and PRM800K-trained PRMs do better — [arXiv 2412.06559](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06559).
- BrokenMath (Petrov, Dekoninck, Vechev, arXiv 2510.04721, 6 Oct 2025): false theorems obtained by LLM perturbation of 2025 competition problems, expert-refined; best model GPT-5 gave sycophantic "proofs" 29 % of the time; test-time interventions and SFT reduce but "do not eliminate" it — [arXiv 2510.04721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04721). MathArena's live BrokenMath page (2026) reports the best model, GPT-5.4, "just under 40 %" on the abstain/refute metric — [matharena.ai/brokenarxiv](https://matharena.ai/brokenarxiv/) (search summary; see Gaps).
- Litmus-Test paper: prompting models to self-reflect on named failure modes gives only "modest improvements" — [arXiv HTML 2506.17114](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17114).
- General LLM-judge literature (not proof-specific): documented biases are position, verbosity, self-preference and leniency; recommended mitigation is a judge from a different model family and, for critical decisions, a three-family ensemble with majority/weighted vote — [futureagi.com 2026](https://futureagi.com/blog/evaluating-llm-judge-bias-mitigation-2026/); self-preference measured in [arXiv 2410.21819](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21819v2) and [arXiv 2506.02592](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.02592); family-conditioned preference in judge panels [arXiv 2609.17857](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.17857) (Sep 2026). Multi-agent debate/jury setups (ChatEval, DEBATE, CourtEval, MAJ-EVAL) report stronger human agreement than single judges — [arXiv 2508.02994 survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02994v1); LLM Jury-on-Demand [arXiv 2512.01786](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2512.01786).

### Inferences
- A realistic planning number for a single strong judge on hard, subtly wrong proofs is a 10–15 % false-accept rate (NVIDIA 7/48; OPC ~10 % overall error), *not* the 3–5 % one might infer from aggregate correlations. Two independent judges with a conjunctive merge (accept only if both accept) would, if errors were independent, drive false accept to ~1–2 %, but the OPC/ProofGrader family-bias results show errors are correlated within a model family — use judges from different families and give them different evidence.
- Rubrics/reference solutions do not fix leniency on their own (USAMO 20× inflation with human rubric; OPC ±3 %). The lever that empirically moves false accepts is *strict step-level checking with a restricted set of permitted premises* — precisely what "name the first unjustified step" enforces — at the cost of more false rejects that a human gate must adjudicate.
- Self-grading is the weakest configuration (own-family bias, BrokenMath sycophancy); a referee that never grades a proof written by its own family removes a documented bias.

### Gaps
- No paper reports a clean false-accept vs false-reject confusion matrix for a *two-judge conjunctive* setup on proofs; the closest are maj@k and median-of-5 numbers.
- The GPT-5.4 "just under 40 %" BrokenMath figure comes from a search summary of the MathArena page, not a fetched primary text.
- I found no 2026 measurement of *length* or *confidence-wording* bias specifically on proof grading; only general LLM-judge bias literature and the USAMO observation that chaotic outputs are under-scored.

## Key question 3 — How did the IMO-gold 2025 systems verify their proofs, and what did human graders say?

### Takeaway
Neither gold system relied on an external checker: DeepMind's Gemini Deep Think used parallel thinking plus RL on proof/grading data and was graded by official IMO coordinators ("clear, precise and most of them easy to follow"), while OpenAI's experimental model used "no tools" and was graded internally by three former IMO medalists with unanimous consensus (not IMO-certified). Independent replications (Huang & Yang; DeepSeekMath-V2) made self-verification explicit via a verifier agent or trained verifier.

### Cited Findings
- DeepMind, 21 Jul 2025: advanced Gemini Deep Think scored 35/42, "officially graded and certified by IMO coordinators" with "the same criteria as for student solutions"; IMO President Gregor Dolinar: "IMO graders found them to be clear, precise and most of them easy to follow"; the IMO board "clarified their review covered answer correctness only — not validation of the system". Method: "parallel thinking… explore and combine multiple possible solutions", RL on "multi-step reasoning, problem-solving and theorem-proving data", curated solution corpus; end-to-end natural language — [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/advanced-version-of-gemini-with-deep-think-officially-achieves-gold-medal-standard-at-the-international-mathematical-olympiad/).
- OpenAI, 19 Jul 2025 (Alexander Wei): "For each problem, three former IMO medalists independently graded the model's submitted proof, with scores finalized after unanimous consensus"; Sébastien Bubeck: "no tools no nothing"; it was OpenAI's own evaluation, not an IMO-certified result — [Simon Willison, 19 Jul 2025](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jul/19/openai-gold-medal-math-olympiad/); [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/openai-model-earns-gold-medal-score-at-international-math-olympiad-and/).
- Huang & Yang, "Winning Gold at IMO 2025 with a Model-Agnostic Verification-and-Refinement Pipeline", arXiv 2507.15855 (Jul 2025): an explicit verifier prompt ("act as an IMO grader… produce a step-by-step verification log") iterated with refinement; 5/6 with Gemini 2.5 Pro (later Grok-4, GPT-5) — [arXiv 2507.15855](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.15855).
- DeepSeekMath-V2, arXiv 2511.22570, 27 Nov 2025: trains a proof *verifier*, uses it as reward for the generator ("identify and resolve as many issues as possible in their own proofs before finalizing"), and a *meta-verification* loop that scales verification compute to auto-label hard proofs; gold-level IMO 2025 and CMO 2024, Putnam 2024 118/120; motto "correct answers don't guarantee correct reasoning" — [arXiv 2511.22570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22570).
- Gemini Deep Think (IMO Gold) as a *grader* scores only 50.2 % exact-grade accuracy on IMO-GradingBench (o3 54.0 %) — [arXiv HTML 2511.01846v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01846v1).
- MathArena's independent IMO 2025 evaluation of *public* models (Jul 2025): best 31 %, human double grading; models produced "bogus citations" — [matharena.ai/imo](https://matharena.ai/imo/).
- Open recipe replication (NVIDIA Nemotron, arXiv 2609.10712, Sep 2026) exists — [arXiv 2609.10712](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10712) (title only; not fetched).

### Inferences
- The gold systems' "verification" was internal to generation (RL-trained rigor, parallel candidates), and certification came from humans; the gap between generation ability (gold) and grading ability (~50 % exact-grade) in the same Gemini system supports keeping a human gate after any automatic referee.
- Verifier-in-the-loop pipelines (Huang & Yang; DeepSeekMath-V2) are the public precedent for a "judge A names the flaw, generator repairs" loop.

### Gaps
- OpenAI has not published its graders' notes or rubric; DeepMind has not published per-step grader comments. Claims about "what graders said" are limited to the two quotes above.

## Key question 4 — Protocols and taxonomies for a planted-error evaluation set

### Takeaway
Published taxonomies converge on ~10 categories (Litmus Test: transformation/target mismatch, over-generalization, invalid construction, wrong/incomplete case division, circular reasoning, logic violation, hidden assumption, boundary neglect, vague argument, incomplete proof; MathArena: logic / assumption / creativity; ProofVerifier: unjustified assumption 15.5 %, incomplete logic flow 56.7 %, made-up conclusion 27.8 %). Planting methods in use: LLM perturbation + expert refinement (BrokenMath), minimal single-point surgical edits (FormalRewardBench), curated valid/invalid research proofs (FirstProof suite), and citation-hallucination typologies (HALLMARK, 14 types).

### Cited Findings
- Litmus Test taxonomy (10 + Others): Transformation Error, Over Generalization, Invalid Construction, Wrong Division, Circular Reasoning, Logic Violation, Hidden Assumption, Boundary Neglect, Vague Argument, Incomplete Proof; dominant in practice: Logic Violation, Hidden Assumption, Vague Argument, Incomplete Proof, rising with difficulty; annotation by consensus of six CS researchers with competition background — [arXiv HTML 2506.17114](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17114).
- MathArena USAMO first-failure taxonomy: Logic (fallacies/unjustified leaps), Assumption (unproven or incorrect assumptions), Creativity (wrong strategy); plus behaviours: forcing boxed answers, generalising from small cases "without providing a formal proof", all models "consistently claimed to have solved the problems" — [usamo_report.pdf §3.2, App. C](https://files.sri.inf.ethz.ch/matharena/usamo_report.pdf).
- ProofVerifier negative-sample taxonomy with frequencies: misusing unjustified assumptions 15.5 %, incomplete logic flow 56.7 %, making up non-existent conclusions 27.8 %; appendix also lists hallucinated theorems and overlooked preconditions — [arXiv HTML 2602.02377](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02377).
- ComBench (olympiad combinatorics, arXiv 2606.10479, Jun 2026) uses categories including "Missing Core Mechanism" and "False Lemma" — [arXiv 2606.10479](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.10479) (search summary).
- Path-planning optimality proofs benchmark (arXiv 2603.19464, Mar 2026): logical errors = overgeneralization, oversimplification, missing edge cases, skipped computations, *uncited nonstandard results*, wrong inequality direction, unsupported inference; separate class = hallucination errors — [arXiv 2603.19464](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.19464) (search summary).
- FormalRewardBench (arXiv 2605.10141, May 2026, formal proofs): error injection via minimal variation, misleading comments, Python-generated variants and direct incorrect generation; "minimal, surgical single-point modifications with maximal semantic impact" — [arXiv 2605.10141](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.10141) (search summary).
- BrokenMath construction: perturb real 2025 problems into plausible false statements with an LLM, then expert review to ensure well-formed falsity — [arXiv 2510.04721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04721).
- FirstProof-based suite: 21 research-grade proofs labelled strictly valid / explicitly invalid, mixing official solutions, Aletheia-generated correct proofs and OpenAI submissions — [arXiv 2606.10799](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10799); OpenAI's submissions page [openai.com/index/first-proof-submissions](https://openai.com/index/first-proof-submissions/).
- HALLMARK (Reizinger & Brendel, arXiv 2607.18360, 20 Jul 2026): 2,526 BibTeX entries, 14 hallucination types, 3 difficulty levels, 6 diagnostic sub-tests per entry, held-out contamination-resistant split; key finding "false-positive rate, not recall, decides whether a verifier is deployable"; tool-augmented lookup raises recall *and* false positives; models over-flag post-cutoff papers — [arXiv 2607.18360](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18360).
- ProcessBench protocol (earliest-error-step label, human-annotated, 3,400 cases) is the standard format for step-localisation evaluation — [arXiv 2412.06559](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06559).
- Label-quality protocol from ProofVerifier: unanimous 5-of-5 agreement across 3 frontier models, then stratified human audit per (source, prompt, generator) slice with escalating agreement thresholds — [arXiv HTML 2602.02377](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02377).

### Inferences
- A planted-error set for a two-judge referee should stratify by (a) the Litmus/ProofVerifier logical categories for judge A (target mismatch = "Transformation Error", missing case = "Wrong Division"/"Boundary Neglect", circular reasoning, wrong lemma = "Hidden Assumption"/"False Lemma", unjustified computation = "Vague Argument"/skipped computation) and (b) provenance categories for judge B (fabricated or mis-attributed citation per HALLMARK types; code whose claimed output does not reproduce; exhaustive search whose stated domain is smaller than the claim). Score *earliest-error localisation* (ProcessBench) not just accept/reject.
- Balance the set: ProofVerifier's 56.7 % "incomplete logic flow" suggests generation-native errors skew toward gaps rather than outright false steps; add surgical single-point edits (FormalRewardBench style) so that subtle wrong steps are represented.
- Report false-accept and false-reject separately and at the realistic base rate of wrong submissions, following HALLMARK's argument that FPR governs usefulness.

### Gaps
- No published planted-error set combines mathematical and provenance errors in one proof; the team would be building the first such set as far as this search found.
- Exact per-category frequencies from the Litmus Test paper and from the USAMO Figure 2 were not extractable as numbers here.

## Key question 5 — Prior work separating logical review from evidence/provenance review, or re-executing code as part of grading

### Takeaway
I found no published proof-grading system that formally splits "mathematical correctness" from "evidence/provenance" as two independent judges. The nearest analogues are: (i) HALLMARK-style citation verifiers (separate task, FPR-dominated); (ii) the Erdős-problems community protocol that separates informal argument, formal Lean proof, and human sign-off that the formal statement matches the claim; (iii) FrontierMath and MathArena using code/answer checks for answer problems but humans for proofs; (iv) the LLM-as-a-Verifier framework, which explicitly does *not* re-execute code. Re-execution of submitted code as a grading step appears only in software benchmarks, not in math-proof grading.

### Cited Findings
- LLM-as-a-Verifier (Kwok, Li, Atreya, Liu, Jiang, Finn, Pavone, Stoica, Mirhoseini; Stanford/Berkeley/NVIDIA; arXiv 2607.05391v1, 6 Jul 2026): post-hoc LLM scoring with continuous logit-expectation scores and criteria decomposition (specification, output format, error detection); "does not re-execute code"; "no separate logical/evidential checks"; accuracies Terminal-Bench V2 86.5 %, SWE-Bench Verified 78.2 %, MedAgentBench 73.3 % — [arXiv HTML 2607.05391v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05391v1).
- HALLMARK citation verification: tool-augmented lookup trades recall for false positives; FPR is decisive — [arXiv 2607.18360](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18360).
- Erdős-problems forum ("AI Contributions" thread, 2026): Will Blair describes automated checks that read "what the kernel already established when the proof compiled" (axioms, hypotheses as parameters) with human sign-off that formal statements match informal claims; found "nine problems recorded as solved hold only under an unproven result"; Problem 650 case: Lean proof compiled, informal argument had a gap, formalisation "closed that gap without disclosure" (found by Woett and Tang); Nat Sothanaphan screens by comparing informal vs formal proofs and asking GPT for argument overviews — [erdosproblems.com forum](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/AI%20Contributions).
- Provenance failure episode, Oct 2025: Bubeck claimed GPT-5-pro "solved" Erdős #339 (it had located a 20-year-old solution); Kevin Weil's deleted post claimed "solutions to 10 previously unsolved Erdős problems"; Thomas Bloom: the posts "were seriously inconsistent with the facts", GPT-5 "just found some references"; Hassabis called it "embarrassing" — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/leading-openai-researcher-announced-a-gpt-5-math-breakthrough-that-never-happened/); [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/openai-math-hype/).
- Statement autoformalisation is itself unreliable as an evidence check: "Beyond Compilation" (arXiv 2606.31002, Jun 2026) finds compile–faithfulness gaps of 3.0–29.0 pp; a GPT-5.2 tool-augmented agent compiled 89.5 % but met semantic criteria on only 60.5 % — [arXiv 2606.31002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31002). Robustness of proof autoformalisation to perturbations: [arXiv 2606.14867](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.14867).
- Boldt, "Explanations, Prompts, and Formalizations", arXiv 2608.29401, 29 Aug 2026: proposes norms — disclose prompts and software setup, formalise for machine verification, supply human explanations — [arXiv 2608.29401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29401).
- Epoch AI: automatic verification for FrontierMath answer problems, manual grading for proofs; small LLM verifiers rejected as biased/inaccurate — [epoch.ai](https://epoch.ai/frontiermath/tiers-1-4) (search summary).
- Consequence-based grading (arXiv 2602.06291) validates a solution indirectly through downstream verifiable problems, a form of "evidence" check without provenance — [arXiv 2602.06291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06291).
- MathArena judges flagged "bogus citations" in IMO 2025 outputs, i.e. provenance errors occur in competition proofs too — [matharena.ai/imo](https://matharena.ai/imo/).

### Inferences
- The team's judge-A/judge-B split has no direct precedent in the proof-grading literature; the closest documented practice is the Erdős community's three-way separation (informal argument, formal proof, statement-match sign-off). The design is therefore defensible but must be validated in-house.
- Re-executing submitted code is standard in SWE benchmarks but absent from every math-grading paper found; including it is a genuine differentiator, and the Erdős "closed the gap without disclosure" case shows why matching the *stated* scope of a computation to the *claimed* theorem is as important as re-running it.
- HALLMARK's lesson transfers to judge B: measure and cap its false-positive rate on genuine citations, or its flags will be ignored.

### Gaps
- No paper found that measures LLM accuracy at detecting *unfaithful exhaustive-search claims* (e.g. domain smaller than stated) in informal proofs; this is an open evaluation gap.
- Boldt's search snippet attributed a "judges awarded 0.9/1.0 to hallucinated algebraic properties" anecdote to that paper; the abstract does not contain it and I could not confirm the origin. Treat as unverified.

## Key question 6 — What experts say about the trustworthiness of LLM-graded proofs

### Takeaway
Expert consensus (Tao, MathArena, Epoch, DeepMind, Berkeley/ProofBench authors) as of mid-2026: LLM graders are useful for triage and best-of-n selection but are not a substitute for human or formal verification; the dominant failure is fluent, plausible, wrong output that judges reward, so independent verification and human sign-off remain mandatory.

### Cited Findings
- Tao: "in math, we can completely check and verify outputs, and this really filters out a lot of the rubbish" (IEEE Spectrum, Jun 2026); "I would caution against using AI tools without the ability to independently verify their output" (blog comments, Nov 2025); LLMs "do not rate their own confidence accurately" (The Atlantic, Feb 2026); "A stronger case for AI in 'red teaming' than 'blue teaming'" (Mastodon, 25 Jul 2025); humans author theorem statements, automation the proofs, with "unit tests" to catch misformalisation (blog, Mar 2026) — [Tao AI views summary](https://teorth.github.io/tao-web/ai-views.html).
- Tao (video, mid-2025): AI proofs "look superficially flawless… the errors are often really subtle and then when you spot them, they're really stupid" — [X/@nabeelqu](https://x.com/nabeelqu/status/1934285488614945035) (secondary quotation).
- MathArena (USAMO 2025): LLM graders inflated scores up to 20× even with human rubric and verified solution — [usamo_report.pdf](https://files.sri.inf.ethz.ch/matharena/usamo_report.pdf); MathArena/BrokenMath: LLMs "prefer to bluff and produce incorrect proofs rather than abstain" — [matharena.ai/brokenarxiv](https://matharena.ai/brokenarxiv/).
- DeepMind IMO-Bench: automatic grading "not yet a perfect substitute for human experts" — [arXiv HTML 2511.01846v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01846v1).
- NVIDIA: "strong verifiers still cannot be fully trusted for rigorous proof evaluation" — [arXiv HTML 2511.13027v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13027v1).
- Epoch AI: declined LLM correctness judges for Tier 4 proofs due to inaccuracy and model-favouring bias — [epoch.ai](https://epoch.ai/frontiermath/tiers-1-4) (search summary).
- IMO board on Gemini: certification "covered answer correctness only — not validation of the system" — [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/advanced-version-of-gemini-with-deep-think-officially-achieves-gold-medal-standard-at-the-international-mathematical-olympiad/).
- Thomas Bloom on the Oct 2025 Erdős claims: "seriously inconsistent with the facts" — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/leading-openai-researcher-announced-a-gpt-5-math-breakthrough-that-never-happened/).

### Inferences
- The team's "human approval as final gate" matches every expert position found; the literature supports using the automatic referee to (1) reject early with a named step and (2) rank candidates, not to certify.
- Tao's "red-team not blue-team" framing is a direct argument for judge A's mandate ("name the first unjustified step") over a holistic score.

### Gaps
- I did not locate a single consolidated 2026 statement from MathArena or Epoch specifically about *two-judge* or *evidence-checking* referees; statements are about single LLM graders.
